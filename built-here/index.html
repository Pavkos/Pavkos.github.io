Car loan interest deduction check for iPhone

Is your car made in the USA? Scan your VIN and find out in a minute whether your car loan interest may qualify for the federal deduction for tax years 2025–2028.

Estimate — not tax advice. Not affiliated with the IRS, NHTSA or any government agency. Rules can change: check IRS.gov or a tax professional before you file.

What the app does

  • VIN check (free): scan or type your VIN. The app looks it up in NHTSA's public vPIC database and shows where the vehicle had its final assembly and its weight class.
  • Eligibility (free): a few questions (new vehicle, loan, loan date, lien, personal use, refinance) give a verdict: likely eligible, not eligible or needs info, with the reasons. Any number of vehicles.
  • Tax Pack (one-time purchase): the deduction estimate with the $10,000 cap and the income phase-out, a one-page PDF summary for Schedule 1-A or your tax preparer, and optional tax-time reminders.

Support

The scanner doesn't read my VIN

Try the barcode on the driver's door jamb in good light, or the VIN through the bottom of the windshield. You can always type the VIN instead. If the check digit doesn't match, the app asks you to confirm or correct the VIN.

NHTSA has no plant data for my VIN

Check “Final Assembly Point” on the window sticker (Monroney label) and answer the question in the app.

The interest amount differs from my lender's statement

The app's estimate uses standard monthly amortization. Many auto loans use daily simple interest, so your lender's year-end statement (Form 1098-VLI) is the number to file. Turn on “I have the actual amount from my lender” on the Loan screen and enter it.

I bought the Tax Pack on another device

Open Settings in the app and tap Restore Purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account.

I already filed my 2025 return

You may be able to amend it with Form 1040-X. Ask a tax professional.
